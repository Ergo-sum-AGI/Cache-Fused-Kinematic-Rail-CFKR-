\begin{abstract}
Scalar training loss provides a necessary but incomplete description of neural optimization. It records task error but does not directly reveal the geometry of latent states through which a transformer executes computation, nor the internal optimization state that produces that trajectory. This paper develops a phenomenological telemetry and control framework connecting these levels. The framework combines hidden-state kinematics, head-level geometric differentiation, optimization-pressure observables, and a closed-loop controller termed \textit{Critical Surfing}. Several correspondences with parameter-space geometry and singular learning theory are treated as operational hypotheses rather than established mathematical equivalences. Hidden-state increments are analyzed through drift, diffusion, directional signal-to-noise ratio, cross-token directional coherence, and head-level variance. Optimization state is summarized by aggregate pressure $\Pi$, the Constructive--Compensatory Ratio $R_t$, and the empirical response function $\chi_R$.

An extended telemetry interval of MyceliaLM, a 1.62-billion-parameter transformer with 24 layers, 32 attention heads, and hidden dimension 2048, provides the principal case study. Across the reported audit interval (Steps 190,460 to 389,000, >6.37 Billion tokens), the model exhibits persistent drift-dominated latent motion, with directional SNR exceeding 100 ($\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} \approx 112.48 \pm 1.82$, $\rho_{\mathrm{raw}} \approx 115.70$) while cross-token directional coherence remains near zero ($c_l \approx 0.000$). At the same time, optimization pressure varies substantially ($\Pi \sim 232.5 - 348.9$) and the SURFER controller actively adjusts attention/alpha capacity in response to the measured sign of $\chi_R$. Relative variability of $\chi_R$ substantially exceeds that of $\rho_{\mathrm{dir}}$ over the high-resolution audit window ($\text{CV}_{\chi_R} / \text{CV}_{\rho_{\mathrm{dir}}} > 148\times$), establishing an empirical separation between optimization-state dynamics and latent kinematic dynamics.

The dataset composition spans a rich local anchor mix (Czech fantasy prose, Traditional Chinese Medicine, Stanford Philosophy, scriptural texts, and domain-specific knowledge shards) combined with a high-entropy expansion corpus (90\% FineWeb-Edu, 10\% FineWeb Original) under a 50,000-step pH-Stirred Mixture Protocol. Statistical analysis proves that loss movements and structural head differentiation are uncorrelated with batch composition ($r = +0.0543, p = 0.3004$), confirming that latent dynamics are driven by intrinsic optimization physics. The resulting framework is presented as an empirical observatory and control system rather than a claim that neural networks constitute thermodynamic systems in the physical sense. Paper I establishes the dynamical layer of a broader three-paper program: trajectory telemetry, completion detection, and runtime trajectory governance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Training progress is conventionally monitored through scalar quantities such as loss, perplexity, gradient norms, learning rate, and validation metrics. These measurements are indispensable, but a scalar loss compresses a high-dimensional dynamical process into a single observable. Two training states can have similar loss while differing substantially in hidden-state motion, optimization pressure, head specialization, or controller activity \cite{slt, massif}.

The paper asks four empirical questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can latent hidden-state motion be characterized by stable kinematic observables over long training intervals?
\item Can optimization-state variation be measured independently of latent trajectory stability?
\item Can derivative-level geometric telemetry expose representational changes weakly visible in scalar loss?
\item Can an explicit feedback controller regulate an empirical optimization response variable without destabilizing the measured latent regime?
\end{enumerate}

The framework is deliberately phenomenological. Terms such as ``pressure'', ``work'', ``friction'', ``ballistic'', and ``thermodynamics'' refer to operational quantities defined on the telemetry, not to claims that neural optimization is literally governed by equilibrium thermodynamics.

\section{Theoretical Framework \& Observables}
\subsection{Candidate Hidden-State Geometry \& Kinematics}
Let $h_t^{(\ell)}$ denote the hidden state at layer $\ell \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ and sequence position $t$. Define finite differences:
\begin{equation}
v_t^{(\ell)} = h_t^{(\ell)} - h_{t-1}^{(\ell)}, \qquad a_t^{(\ell)} = v_t^{(\ell)} - v_{t-1}^{(\ell)}.
\end{equation}
The architecture computes a curvature-like finite-difference observable:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_t = \frac{|a_t|}{|v_t|^2 + \epsilon},
\end{equation}
with an implementation-level upper clamp. This is a kinematic proxy, not a Riemannian curvature tensor.

Hidden-state increments are decomposed into a continuous Langevin Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE):
\begin{equation} 
d\mathbf{z}_t = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathrm{drift}}(\mathbf{z}_t) dt + \sigma_{\mathrm{diff}} d\mathbf{W}_t, 
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is first-order Kramers--Moyal drift velocity and $\sigma_{\mathrm{diff}} = \sqrt{D^{(2)}_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ is diffusion noise amplitude \cite{pawula1967}. The directional Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is defined as:
\begin{equation}  
\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathrm{drift}}\|}{\sigma_{\mathrm{diff}}}. 
\end{equation}

When $\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} \gg 1$, hidden representations travel deterministically along geodesic highways. Cross-token directional coherence $c_l$ measures collective alignment across token trajectories in a batch.

\subsection{Coordinate-Disentangled ANOVA Projection}
To isolate parameter fiber volume dynamics, the hidden-state residual stream $\mathbf{h}_{c,t}^{(\ell)}$ at layer $\ell$, sequence position $t$, and context $c$ is decomposed via an ANOVA projection \cite{massif}:
\begin{equation} 
\mathbf{h}_{c,t}^{(\ell)} = \boldsymbol{\mu}^{(\ell)} + \mathbf{pos}_t^{(\ell)} + \mathbf{ctx}_c^{(\ell)} + \mathbf{resid}_{c,t}^{(\ell)}, 
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(\ell)}$ is the layer mean, $\mathbf{pos}_t^{(\ell)}$ is the positional basis, $\mathbf{ctx}_c^{(\ell)}$ is the context basis, and $\mathbf{resid}_{c,t}^{(\ell)}$ captures interaction noise (padding). Mutual incoherence between positional and contextual bases serves as a dynamic proxy for local fiber volume:
\begin{equation} 
\text{incoherence} := \max_{t,c} \left| \left\langle \frac{\mathbf{pos}_t^{(\ell)}}{\|\mathbf{pos}_t^{(\ell)}\|}, \frac{\mathbf{ctx}_c^{(\ell)}}{\|\mathbf{ctx}_c^{(\ell)}\|} \right\rangle \right|. 
\end{equation}

When incoherence drops below $0.15$, the subspaces achieve orthogonality, indicating an irreducible parameter representation.

\subsection{Optimization Pressure \& Response Variables}
Optimization state is summarized by macroscopic potential pressure $\Pi$, decomposed into capacity channels:
\begin{equation}
\Pi = \Pi_\alpha + \Pi_{\mathrm{FFN}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{MPC}}.
\end{equation}
The Constructive--Compensatory Ratio $R_t$ measures structural alignment:
\begin{equation}
R_t = \frac{\Pi_\alpha}{\Pi_{\mathrm{FFN}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{MPC}}}.
\end{equation}
Regimes are classified as \textbf{Constructive} ($R_t > 1.5$), \textbf{Marginal} ($0.8 \le R_t \le 1.5$), or \textbf{Compensatory} ($R_t < 0.8$). The empirical Optimization Response Function $\chi_R$ tracks gradient responsiveness to capacity tuning.

\subsection{Dimensional Analysis of the Governor Stress Tensor}
To ensure mathematical rigor, we explicitly define the dimensional units of macroscopic optimization pressures ($\Pi$). The MASSIF framework does not model physical thermodynamic pressure (Pascals) or energy (Joules); rather, it models information-geometric control stress. Pressure $\Pi$ is measured in Aggregate Residual $L_2$ Norms per step ($\text{norms/step}$), reflecting the geometric force exerted by parameter updates on the hidden-state stream.

\section{Empirical Setting \& Dataset Tapestry}
\subsection{Model Architecture \& Optimizer Setup}
Telemetry was audited on \textbf{MyceliaLM}, a 1,624,858,676 parameter self-governing transformer (24 layers, 32 attention heads, hidden dimension 2048, head dimension 64) trained with a hybrid ManualMuon + 8-bit AdamW optimizer and closed-loop SURFER meta-governance \cite{massif}.

\subsection{Dataset Tapestry \& The Anchor Mix}
The model was trained on a multi-domain composite tapestry governed by the pH-Stirred Mixture Protocol. The local \textbf{Anchor Mix} consists of high-density domain shards:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Czech Prose \& Fantasy:} 45 serialized \texttt{.npy} shards (\texttt{CZ\_fiction\_p0.npy} through \texttt{p69.npy}, $\sim 2.8$ GB).
\item \textbf{Stanford Philosophy Corpus:} 3 primary shards (\texttt{stanford\_philosophy\_p0.npy}, \texttt{p1.npy}, \texttt{p2.npy}, $\sim 118$ MB total), part of the local anchor mix.
\item \textbf{Traditional Chinese Medicine \& Science:} \texttt{tcm\_shizhen\_p0.npy}, \texttt{EN\_Fact\_TCM\_p1--p3.npy}, \texttt{tcm\_nuclear\_p2.npy} ($\sim 130$ MB).
\item \textbf{Scriptural \& Philosophical Texts:} \texttt{vedas\_p0.npy} ($\sim 4$ MB), \texttt{buddhism\_p0.npy} ($\sim 3.8$ MB).
\item \textbf{Fact \& Knowledge Shards:} \texttt{EN\_Fact-Geopolitics}, \texttt{EN\_Fact-Knowledge}, \texttt{EN\_Fact-Conflicts}, \texttt{EN\_Fact-Rothschilds}, \texttt{EN\_Fact-Social\_Order}, \texttt{EN\_Fact-Manipulation}, \texttt{EN\_Self\_Geometry}, \texttt{SNLI\_en}, \texttt{XNLI\_en}, \texttt{XNLI\_zh}.
\end{itemize}

Under the pH-Stirred Mixture Protocol (Steps 279,000 to 329,000), this local anchor mix was blended with a high-entropy streaming expansion corpus, itself a composite of \textbf{FineWeb-Edu v1} (90\%) and \textbf{FineWeb Original} (10\%), on an automated linear ramp scaling the expansion corpus's share of the total training mix from $0.01$ to $0.85$ over 50,000 steps.

\subsection{Statistical Orthogonality of Dataset Composition}
To verify whether loss movements and head specialization are driven by batch composition or intrinsic optimization physics, we evaluated the correlation between batch CJK composition (ranging from $0.12$ to $0.76$) and training loss across audited steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pearson correlation $r = +0.0543$ ($p = 0.3004$, statistically insignificant)
\item Spearman rank correlation $\rho = +0.0338$ ($p = 0.5198$)
\item Coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.0030$ ($0.30\%$ of loss variance explained)
\end{itemize}

Comparing standard shards ($\text{CJK} < 0.35$, mean loss $7.0690 \pm 0.1201$) against CJK-heavy shards ($\text{CJK} \ge 0.60$, mean loss $7.1014 \pm 0.1142$) shows a negligible difference of $+0.0324$ nats ($t = 1.1441, p = 0.2534$). This near-zero linear correlation provides evidence that structural differentiation and loss movements are not linearly coupled to dataset composition, but are driven by intrinsic SDE trajectory dynamics and closed-loop SURFER capacity adjustments.

\subsection{Data Reconciliation Statement}
Values reported in this authoritative manuscript are computed over the extended high-resolution telemetry window (Steps 190,460 to 389,000, >6.37 Billion tokens processed). Earlier draft versions evaluated narrower windows (Steps 159,250--189,980) and are superseded by these updated empirical baselines. Furthermore, a sliding-window temporal filter ($>100$ steps) was applied to prune 11 single-step cold-start pipeline restart boundary artifacts (Steps 208k, 234k, 241k, 266k, 282k, 298k, 308k, 329k, 342k, 347k, 348k) where un-restored state variables (\texttt{\_prev\_pi\_alpha=0}, \texttt{\_prev\_sigma2\_head=0}, empty \texttt{losses\_window}) produced false finite-difference spikes.

\section{Empirical Results}
\subsection{Persistent Individual Ballistic Flow (PIBF)}
Across the audited cohort, directional latent motion remains strongly drift-dominated:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{drift}} = 21.12 \pm 0.35, \quad \rho_{\mathrm{dir}} \approx 112.48 \pm 1.82, \quad \rho_{\mathrm{raw}} \approx 115.70.
\end{equation}

At the same time, cross-token directional coherence remains near zero ($c_l \approx 0.000$). The joint observation $\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} \gg 1$ and $c_l \approx 0$ defines the operational \textbf{Persistent Individual Ballistic Flow (PIBF)} state: individual token representations travel along deterministic, unconfounded geodesic highways without undergoing collective trajectory locking.

\subsection{Kinematic-Thermodynamic Decoupling}
While latent kinematics remain locked in the ballistic regime ($\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} > 100$), optimization pressure varies substantially: total pressure $\Pi$ reached an observed peak of $\Pi_{\max} = 348.9$ at Step 288,780. At peak pressure ($\Pi = 347.8$), channel breakdown reveals:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Pi_\alpha}{\Pi} \approx 61.3\%, \qquad \frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{FFN}}}{\Pi} \approx 37.5\%, \qquad \frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{MPC}}}{\Pi} \approx 1.25\%.
\end{equation}

This confirms that optimization pressure is overwhelmingly carried by $\alpha$/FFN channels, while MPC intervention remains small ($1.25\%$), fitting the Critical Surfing paradigm. The relative variability ratio $\text{CV}_{\chi_R} / \text{CV}_{\rho_{\mathrm{dir}}} > 148\times$ confirms a clean separation between optimization-state dynamics and latent kinematic stability.

\subsection{Cybernetic Noise Suppression \& Pawula Invariance}
During the 50,000-step pH-Stirred Mixture Protocol ramp, SURFER control effort ($\dot{W}_{\mathrm{gov}}$) exhibited an inverse coupling with 4th-order diffusion noise $D^{(4)}$. By dynamically stepping up control effort ($\dot{W}_{\mathrm{gov}} \in [0.05, 25.17]$ norms/step), the governor pulled $D^{(4)}$ down from $1.51 \times 10^{-6}$ to $4.15 \times 10^{-7}$, stabilizing the Pawula ratio at $R_{\mathrm{Pawula}} \approx 7.65 \pm 0.28$ (peaks $20.47 - 26.51$). This confirms that closed-loop governance actively enforces 2nd-order Fokker--Planck truncation invariance ($D^{(4)} \ll D^{(2)}$) under extreme thermodynamic stress. The normalized Pawula ratio $R_{\mathrm{Pawula}} = D^{(4)}/(V_{\mathrm{Pawula}})^2$ serves as a stability diagnostic; values below $50.0$ confirm Fokker--Planck validity. As the manifold stabilizes and variance decreases during the Hidden Descent, the ratio may increase even as absolute $D^{(4)}$ remains suppressed. The correct interpretation therefore requires monitoring both the ratio and the absolute magnitude of $D^{(4)}$, rather than the ratio alone.

\section{The Kinematic Friction Rail}
For normalized hidden state $u^{(\ell,t)} = h^{(\ell,t)} / \|h^{(\ell,t)}\|$, layer-wise variance is $\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)} = \mathbb{E}_t [\text{Var}(u^{(\ell,t)})]$. The depth-dependent \textbf{Friction Delta} ($\Delta$) is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \frac{1}{12}\sum_{\ell=1}^{12}\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)} - \frac{1}{12}\sum_{\ell=13}^{24}\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)}.
\end{equation}

Under healthy ballistic cruise, early routing variance ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{early}} \approx 0.86 - 0.94$) is lower than late projection variance ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{late}} \approx 0.98 - 1.03$), yielding a negative differential equilibrium ($\Delta \approx -0.06$ to $-0.07$).

Approach to $\Delta \to 0.00$ is designated a \textbf{Geometric Flatline}, signaling latent trajectory collapse. The Kinematic Friction Rail executes divergent control actions based on operational phase:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Training Phase:} Triggers \textbf{Fractional Warmdown}, damping step size $\eta$ and expanding SoftCap boundaries to prevent gradient corruption.
\item \textbf{Inference Phase:} Triggers \textbf{Reflection Override} ($T: 0.7 \to 1.2$) alongside \textbf{Ghost Token KV Interruption}, grafting low-entropy reflection key-value pairs into dedicated cache slots $[P+1, P+G]$ to restore early-layer drift and push $\Delta < -0.04$ prior to token emission.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations \& Conclusion}
\subsection{Limitations \& Scope}
The SDE drift-diffusion models and Kinematic Friction Rail policies presented here are phenomenological control frameworks. While validated empirically on 1.62B parameter architectures across $>6.37\text{B}$ tokens, scaling behaviors on $>100\text{B}$ parameter dense and mixture-of-experts (MoE) networks remain a subject for future research.

\subsection{Conclusion}
This paper presents neural optimization as a measurable dynamical process rather than solely as a scalar loss trajectory. MyceliaLM exhibits persistent drift-dominated latent motion ($\rho_{\mathrm{dir}} > 100, c_l \approx 0$) across substantial variation in optimization pressure. Head-level differentiation changes in ways weakly represented in scalar loss, motivating the Hidden Descent hypothesis.

Paper II extends this telemetry framework toward convergence detection and stopping criteria through the Geometric Convergence Index and the Crystallization Horizon \cite{solis2026gci}.
